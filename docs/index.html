FlyRank ML Internship · Capstone Study · Lane 1: Ranking Signal Analysis

Ranking Signal Decline: What Predicts It, and What Doesn't

ML Intern, FlyRank March 2026 dev slice Grouped-validation study

Abstract

This study asks whether pre-decision signals — search position, content depth, and content age — can identify which pages in a content portfolio are losing search performance, without relying on the outcome data used to define decline itself. Using a March 2026 slice of FlyRank's content-performance warehouse, a client-grouped Random Forest classifier was compared against a rule-based baseline and a logistic regression baseline on identical validation splits. The grouped model showed measured discriminative ability (AUC in the 0.79–0.85 range across seeds) but relied heavily on one feature (impressions) later flagged as a soft leakage risk, and its predicted probabilities rarely exceeded 0.53, indicating low absolute confidence even where relative ranking was useful. The resulting output is framed as a decision-support ranking for human review, not an autonomous classifier, with explicit no-go cases for where it should not be trusted.

01 · Introduction

Problem Statement

Content teams cannot manually review every page in a growing portfolio to judge whether it is still performing. FlyRank's own internal research (The State of AI-Driven SEO, March 2026) shows portfolio-wide patterns — content decays after roughly 270 days, refresh timing is one of the strongest measured levers available, and click capture compresses sharply once a page leaves the top search positions. Those findings describe the portfolio in aggregate. This study asks a narrower, page-level question: using only signals that would be known before a review decision is made, can a model usefully rank which individual pages are declining right now — and just as importantly, can it do so without quietly training on the very outcome it is meant to predict?

The decision this supports is editorial triage: which pages should a content team look at first, this cycle. The action is a targeted review — a snippet rewrite, a content refresh, or no action at all. The cost of getting it wrong is real: reviewer hours spent on a page that was never actually a problem, or a genuinely declining page left untouched because it looked unremarkable.

What was excluded, and why

  • The sealed final month (June 2026). This month is the natural forward-looking outcome window for any past→future label built elsewhere in the panel. It was never used to develop label logic or tune thresholds, and remains untouched for a future held-out check.
  • GA4 engagement fields where unavailable. Only 4.2% of rows in this slice carry valid GA4 data; unfiltered inclusion would have injected false zeros as signal rather than genuine absence of engagement.
  • Any column used to construct the label. Raw last-30-day / previous-30-day click and impression columns were excluded from the feature set entirely to avoid the model decoding its own answer key (see Methodology).

03 · Methodology

Approach

Label

No externally verified "decline" label exists in this dataset. A proxy label, is_declining, was constructed by comparing each page's average daily clicks in the first half versus second half of the development month. This is an internally consistent proxy, not ground truth, and is described throughout this paper in those terms.

Features

Five signals, each chosen because it is knowable at the decision moment, independent of the outcome window:

  • avg_position — current search ranking, observed same-day
  • word_count — fixed at time of writing
  • content_age_days — arithmetic from publish date, no future data involved
  • content_type, main_intent — static page attributes
  • impressions_total — same-day visibility (see Limitations for a caveat found on this feature)

Baseline

A rule-based baseline was built first, flagging pages ranked 4–20 with click-through rate below the position-tier average. This baseline was deliberately simple and interpretable, and gave a floor to beat.

Validation design

Content items from the same client likely share writing style, templates, and topical niche. A random row-level split risks placing near-duplicate pages from the same client on both sides of train/test, letting a model learn client identity rather than a generalizable signal. All reported model results therefore use a client-grouped split: no client appears in both the training and test set.

Leakage checks

A deliberate leakage trap was run early in this project: a label-derived column (second-half click volume) was added directly as a feature. Predictive score jumped from an honest 0.670 AUC toward near-perfect separation — confirming the column was effectively decoding the label itself. It was removed and never used in the reported model. A later audit also flagged that impressions_total, while not label-derived, is aggregated over the same window used to build the label, and is treated in the Results and Limitations sections as a softer, second-order leakage risk rather than a clean pre-decision signal.

measured The Random Forest outperformed both baselines on every seed tested, but the spread across seeds (0.785–0.850, a 0.065 range) is larger than the gap originally observed between a naive random split and the grouped split (0.011). This means the earlier appearance of "grouped beats naive" cannot be treated as a stable finding — it falls within normal split-to-split variance, and is reported here as an open question rather than a conclusion.

What the model leans on

directional Permutation importance was dominated by impressions_total, with position, word count, and content metadata contributing little to nothing — in one run, permuting avg_position slightly improved model performance, suggesting the model was not meaningfully using it. Combined with the leakage audit above, this is read as the model leaning on a feature with partial outcome-window overlap, rather than evidence that position is unimportant to decline generally.

Confidence, not just accuracy

Across 24,684 scored pages, predicted decline probability had a median of 0.09 and a maximum of 0.53 — the model rarely expressed strong confidence in either direction. This is why the final output (Section 6) is built as a relative ranking using score percentiles, rather than a hard yes/no classifier at a fixed threshold.

05 · Limitations & Honest Framing

What This Study Does Not Show

Observational, not causal

Every relationship reported here is an association observed in one month of one dataset. None of it establishes that changing a page's word count, position, or freshness will cause a proportional change in its outcome.

Single-month development window

All development occurred on March 2026 only. The sealed June 2026 month has never been used to check whether these patterns hold beyond the development window.

Sparse engagement data

Only 4.2% of rows carry valid GA4 engagement signal in this slice, meaning engagement-based features could not be reliably incorporated.

A data-quality finding, not a modeling one

content_updated_date was found dated after the corresponding report date for the large majority of rows in this slice, making a staleness/freshness signal unusable here — despite freshness being a strong, well-supported lever in FlyRank's own portfolio-level research. This is reported as a limitation of this specific data slice, not a contradiction of that broader finding.

Given these constraints, every claim in this paper is phrased as observed, measured, or directional rather than proven, and the model's output is positioned as decision-support for a human reviewer, never as an autonomous judgment of content quality.

06 · Ranked Recommendations

Content Action Playbook

Pages were grouped into five archetypes using the model's predicted decline score (relative to its own distribution, not a fixed external threshold) combined with position and traffic volume.

1 visible_but_at_risk ctr_and_snippet_review

Already ranks on page one but shows elevated decline score. Protect existing visibility first — the highest-value, lowest-effort intervention per position-vs-CTR findings.

2 striking_distance_declining content_refresh

Ranks just outside page one (positions 11–20) with elevated decline score. Refresh plus internal linking to push toward page one, the highest-ROI optimization zone per position-tier click capture.

3 low_volume_unproven monitor_only

Fewer than 50 impressions in the window — insufficient volume to trust the decline signal. This is the largest bucket (52% of scored pages); a separate, lighter-weight process is recommended for this tail rather than the same review pipeline.

4 stable_healthy / watch_list no_action / quarterly_review

Low or ambiguous decline score. No immediate action; revisit on a standing quarterly cycle rather than this cycle's priority queue.

Human review rules

  • Every visible_but_at_risk and striking_distance_declining flag requires human sign-off before any edit is made — the model ranks candidates, it does not approve changes.
  • Pages near the 50-impression floor should be manually checked before being written off as low-volume, in case a recently published or seasonal page was miscategorized.

What should not be automated

  • No auto-publishing or auto-editing of content based on model output.
  • No automatic deprioritization of "stable" pages — false negatives are known to occur, so a quiet model output is not proof a page is fine.
  • No cross-client generalization without re-validation on that client's own content mix.
  • No revenue or ROI claims — this project never had revenue data.

Monitoring and retrain triggers

  • Re-score monthly as new partitions land; a single month may not capture seasonal variation.
  • If grouped-split AUC on a new month drops meaningfully below this study's range, treat it as a signal that underlying relationships have shifted (e.g. a search algorithm update) and re-audit.
  • Before any production use, validate once against the sealed June 2026 month, which has not been touched during development.
